# Decision cost and analysis paralysis

Evidence discipline must be worth its cost. More hypotheses, text, and tool calls are not benefits themselves.

Before expanding an investigation, identify the decision, evidence that could change it, the cheapest accessible discriminator, cost of error, and cost of delay. Respect the user's budget; otherwise choose a proportionate short pass without asking permission for routine choices.

## Stop rules

For reversible local decisions a short check may suffice. For costly or irreversible ones spend the budget on material evidence and recovery boundaries, not an exhaustive inventory.

Stop the current analysis when any applies:

- Evidence supports the next authorized action, even without a final root cause.
- Alternatives lead to the same low-risk action.
- Another pass yields no evidence, distinct prediction, or changed action.
- Useful information requires execution or a named unavailable source.
- The agreed time, cost, or evidence budget expires.

A budget does not lower the standard for claiming facts or authorize risky guesses. If a consequential action remains unsupported, identify the unresolved decision and needed evidence; continue independent work or authorized containment.

## Exit and reopen

Use a few lines: current model and scope; material uncertainty; action or waiting condition; new evidence that would reopen. Continue implementation and necessary validation when justified.

Do not reopen merely because wording changed, someone agrees, or another remote possibility can be imagined. Reopen when new evidence, context, risk, or goals could change action.

## Evaluate the protocol's cost

Record time, input/output size, tool calls, clarification turns, completion, regressions, and whether tests were executed. A baseline reaching the same sound decision with less effort can be preferable.

Urgent authorized containment can precede diagnosis. Label it mitigation, preserve useful evidence if feasible, and resume diagnosis when it can improve the next decision.
